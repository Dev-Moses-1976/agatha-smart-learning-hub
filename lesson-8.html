Section 8 of 10

Troubleshooting hardware problems

Find and identify faults systematically.

1. Reading beep codes and symptoms

When a computer starts it runs the power-on self-test (POST). One short beep usually means success; other patterns signal a fault, but the meanings differ by BIOS maker (AMI, Award, Phoenix), so check the board manual. Continuous or repeating beeps often point to memory or power problems. Typical symptoms: no power (check the cable, outlet and PSU); powers on but no display (check the monitor cable and reseat the RAM); random restarts (overheating or a failing PSU); blue screens (RAM, drivers or disk); clicking noises (a failing hard drive).

2. Isolating a failing part

  • Change one thing at a time and note the result.
  • Reseat RAM, cards and cables first.
  • Boot with only the essentials: CPU, one RAM stick, PSU and graphics.
  • Swap in a known-good part to compare.
  • Use tools: a PSU tester, memory test software, the drive’s SMART health status and the Windows Event Viewer.

3. Common faults and fixes

  • Clock resets or settings lost: replace the CR2032 CMOS battery (about 3 V).
  • Overheating and loud fans: clean the dust and replace dried thermal paste.
  • Random shutdowns: test or replace the PSU.
  • Crashes and blue screens: test each RAM stick.
  • Slow PC with clicking disk: back up at once and replace the drive.
  • Laptop not charging: test the charger, the charging port and the battery.
