The motherboard and internal components

How the motherboard connects everything.

1. Motherboard layout

Find these parts on any board: the CPU socket, RAM slots, PCIe slots, SATA and M.2 connectors, the chipset, the BIOS/UEFI chip with its round CR2032 coin battery (it keeps the clock and settings), the 24-pin power connector, front-panel headers (power button, LEDs, USB) and the rear I/O ports.

Common sizes (form factors): ATX (305 × 244 mm), micro-ATX (244 × 244 mm) and Mini-ITX (170 × 170 mm). The case must support the board’s size.

2. CPU, RAM and expansion slots

  • CPU: the socket must match the processor. Line up the small triangle marker on the CPU with the one on the socket, and never force it. Add a pea-sized drop of thermal paste before fitting the cooler.
  • RAM: DDR3, DDR4 and DDR5 are not interchangeable (the notch sits in a different place). Use matching sticks in the correct slots for dual-channel speed, and press until the side clips click.
  • PCIe x16 slot: for the graphics card; smaller PCIe slots take network, sound or storage cards.

3. Power supply and cooling

The PSU supplies +3.3 V, +5 V and +12 V lines through its cables. Choose a PSU with enough wattage for the parts, from a reputable brand. Cooling removes heat: a heatsink and fan on the CPU, plus case fans. A good airflow pattern draws cool air in at the front or bottom and exhausts hot air at the rear and top. CPUs usually idle at roughly 30–50 °C; sustained temperatures above about 85–90 °C under load mean poor cooling, and the CPU will slow itself down (thermal throttling) to protect itself.
